# The Levi Playbook: hunting characteristics, not EBITDA

Every fund runs the same screen: "$2–10M EBITDA, founder-owned, region X". By the time a company passes
it, a banker is already calling 40 funds. Levi screens on the **leading indicators** that show up online
12–36 months before the financials do. It asks: *which businesses are about to cross our threshold,
and who isn't talking to them yet?*

## The characteristics Levi hunts for

You named the first five. The rest are where an edge actually comes from, because almost nobody
collects them systematically.

### A. Customer love
1. **Strong reviews.** A high rating (Bayesian-adjusted so 5.0 from 12 reviews doesn't beat 4.8 from 9,000) and a high review count.
2. **Review velocity.** Reviews per month, and whether that rate is accelerating. Velocity leads revenue. A 4.7-star business going from 20 to 80 reviews a month is scaling. *Levi snapshots counts over time to measure this.*
3. **Repeat and loyalty language** in reviews ("my 5th order", "we come every week"). This is recurring revenue in disguise.

### B. Attention and brand heat
4. **Strong social presence.** Follower count across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube. **Growth rate** matters more than size, and so does engagement.
5. **Trending right now.** Rising branded Google search interest, press velocity, and viral moments (a celebrity sighting, lines out the door).
6. **Community recommendations.** Unprompted Reddit, forum and YouTube mentions. These are the purest signal of product love, and they are also what AI models learn from.

### C. Discoverability
7. **Google ranking.** Page-one organic rank and local map-pack presence for "<category> <city>".
8. **ChatGPT and AI share of voice.** How often ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity or Gemini name the company when asked "what's the best X". Levi measures this directly. As a bonus, names the AI recommends that Levi isn't tracking yet become free discovery leads. Watch for companies optimizing for AI search (Anchor Heating & Air publishes an `/llm-info` page). That is a tell of savvy management.

### D. Operating velocity (the scaling tells)
9. **Revenue or system-sales growth**, from on-record figures, Inc. 5000 three-year CAGR, or Technomic/RB Future 50.
10. **Unit and door growth.** New locations, signed franchise development agreements, new retail doors (a Costco, Target or Whole Foods win is a step-change).
11. **Hiring mix.** Ops, general-manager and district-manager roles mean the business is scaling. **A first CFO, controller or FP&A hire is a classic pre-transaction tell**: the founder is getting the books investor-ready.
12. **Growth lists.** Inc. 5000, Deloitte Fast 500, Future 50, Entrepreneur Franchise 500, PCT Top 100 and G2 badges. These are *verified* growth data, for free.
13. **Systems upgrades.** Moving to Shopify Plus or NetSuite, or adding Klaviyo or ServiceTitan, means the business outgrew small-business tooling (often around $10M+ revenue).
14. **Trademark filings and paid-ad activity.** These point to new product lines or geographies and a working acquisition engine.

### E. Product and moat
15. **Good talking points.** A one-sentence reason people tell friends ("the sardines in the cute tins", "the skort Taylor Swift wore").
16. **A recurring model.** Subscriptions, memberships, consumables or service contracts.
17. **Category tailwind.** For example Yemeni coffee (the segment grew 50% in 2025), sauna and recovery, Pilates, IV and longevity, non-toxic home, tinned fish.
18. **Pricing power.** A premium price with ratings holding.

### F. Deal-ability (this is where the alpha is)
19. **Founder or family owned**, with little or no institutional capital.
20. **Low institutional awareness.** No banker process, no PE coverage, no "exploring a sale" press. **Levi Alpha** discounts the score of companies that are already shopped.
21. **Succession signals.** Long founder tenure, "next chapter" language, a second generation that may not want to run it.
22. **Fragmented market.** Room to buy and build.

### G. Financial fit, including future fit
23. **Estimated revenue in the fund's band today, or within ~24 months at the current growth rate** (trajectory fit). This is how Levi finds companies *before* they meet the threshold.

### H. Risk flags (subtract)
24. Fad shape (a spike with no baseline), review-integrity anomalies, lawsuits or regulatory issues (BBB complaints, franchisee arbitration, FDA/FTC), key-person dependency (an influencer-founder).

## Financial statements: what is actually obtainable

Private companies don't publish statements. Levi is honest about that, and goes after every public
source that *does* exist before falling back to estimates:

| Source | What you get | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD)** via state registries (WI DFI, MN CARDS, CA DFPI) | **Audited franchisor financial statements (Item 21)**, unit-level revenue (Item 19), openings and closures (Item 20) | Every franchisor; a goldmine that is free and public |
| Inc. 5000 | Verified revenue and 3-year growth | Applicants |
| Founder interviews, podcasts, press | On-record revenue ("we did $50M in 2025") | Many founder-led brands |
| Technomic / RB Future 50 | System sales and growth | Emerging restaurant chains |
| PPP loan data (2020) | Payroll baseline (headcount × wage proxy) | Most small businesses |
| USAspending, state procurement | Contract revenue | Government suppliers |
| CMS / NPI data | Provider counts and Medicare billing | Healthcare practices |
| UCC filings | Who is lending to them, and when | Everyone (signals growth financing) |

Levi then **triangulates**: on-record > Inc. band > system sales > units × AUV > headcount × revenue per
employee > reviews → customers × ticket. Each method gets a range and a confidence, and they are blended
in log space. The indicative P&L (gross margin, EBITDA) uses industry benchmarks and is **always labelled
as such**. Real statements come under NDA, and Levi's job is to get you to that conversation first.
